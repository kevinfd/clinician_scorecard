# Clinician Scorecard: User Journeys

Version 1.1 · 2026-09-28 · Revised after audit (see "Review log") · Derived from `docs/source/metric-definitions-v0.2.md` (the brief) and `docs/designs/clinician-scorecard.md` (the office-hours design doc). Synthesized from four proposals and a three-judge panel; see "Provenance".

## Purpose

This document is the canonical set of user journeys for the Clinician Scorecard. Four journeys cover every metric in the brief, all six ground rules, the steward work that makes the numbers trustworthy, and the dispute path that the design doc says is the product. Each step is written so a builder can derive a feature from it. Where the brief is silent, the step says so and the question goes to the brief's owner rather than being answered here.

## How to read this

- Journeys are numbered J1 to J4. Steps are numbered J1.1, J1.2 and so on. `02-features.md` and the PRD cite these IDs; do not renumber.
- Metric names are quoted exactly as the brief writes them, including "Work RVUs — live tracker" with its dash and the quoted survey items.
- "Proposed default" marks a behavior the brief does not specify and this document proposes so the build can proceed. Every proposed default has a matching Open Question for the brief's owner.
- The wedge referenced throughout is the design doc's revised one: one site, all neurosurgeons, periop's case-level extract, four metrics, email for months one to three. The earlier "one subspecialty, five OR-log metrics" framing was rejected in the design doc's adversarial review (challenges 5 and 24) and is not used here.
- The six ground rules are named as: (1) single-clinician attribution; (2) dispute to chief, then chair; (3) min-n and peer-under-five suppression with the reason shown; (4) anonymous spread; (5) definition and record drill-down; (6) trend.
- J1 and J4 are each written in two parts with two triggers (J1: the monthly email, then a quarter close or the annual review; J4: a period close, then a definition, source or roster change). Each is two journeys under one number. They share one ID because the journey cap is four and each pair has one actor and one set of screens. J2 and J3 have one trigger each.

## J1 — Month posted: the email lands, the surgeon checks their own list, then the first peer spread renders

**Primary actor:** Attending neurosurgeon (any subspecialty, one pilot site).

**Secondary actors:** Department analyst (runs the batch that produces the email, J4); the brief's owner or chair (presents step zero); division chief (receives disputes, J2).

**Trigger:** Part A (wedge, months one to three): the monthly email arrives in the surgeon's MGB mailbox within 10 business days of month close, after the step-zero faculty meeting. Part B (later feeds): a quarter closes with Vizient and QI data loaded, or the fiscal year is closing and the surgeon is preparing for annual review.

**Frequency:** Monthly for Part A. Part B is quarterly, with a fiscal-year-end pass and a second look after billing lag clears. No step assumes the surgeon browses a dashboard unprompted; every visit starts from an email or a review date.

**Preconditions (data that must exist):**
- Step zero held: definitions, ground rules and the one-page "what this is not" presented at faculty meeting; attendance recorded per surgeon; opt-out recorded per surgeon (design doc Distribution Plan, Premise 11).
- Periop case-level extract for the month loaded, reconciled to periop's own report, and published (J4.1 to J4.9).
- Dated department roster with site and subspecialty per surgeon as of the period.
- Definitions as versioned code, with the institutional FCOT and cancellation definitions adopted verbatim.
- Privacy-office confirmation that a surgeon's own case list may be emailed to their MGB mailbox (design doc Premise 7).
- For Part B: the later feeds listed in "Delivery sequencing" and their governance (medical staff office decision before any quality metric is shown).

**Success outcome:** In month one a surgeon reads four numbers that match periop's report, opens their own case list, finds the late case from the 14th and sees why it was counted, and every cell without a number says why. In month two the spread renders for attendees with the peer count shown, and the surgeon who recognizes a colleague is not surprised because step zero said so. At annual review the surgeon knows which numbers are final, what "no evidence of difference" means, and can point to the record behind anything the chief raises.

**Failure modes (what goes wrong and what the user sees):**
- The extract lacks wheels-in or the delay reason per case; FCOT cannot be computed as defined or disputed as promised. The user sees a tile with a reason ("delay reason not in extract"), never a number computed from a proxy.
- The department's FCOT differs from periop's because the definition was not adopted verbatim; the user sees two numbers for the same month and believes neither.
- Co-surgeon cases are common; the first thing a surgeon finds in their list is a case they did not lead. Survivable only if J2 works and the "shared" flag is visible on the row.
- A roster error puts a surgeon in the wrong subspecialty or site; a spread renders that should have suppressed, or suppresses with a false reason.
- The extract carries fewer than 12 months; the trend is one or two points on day one. The tile must say "history from <date>".
- A feed fails and OR case volume publishes as 0; the user reads it as a real month (guarded in J4.8).
- An in-progress month is shown without a cue and the surgeon over-reacts to a 2-of-3 FCOT month. Default is closed months only.
- The peer-under-five rule is read literally and the surgeon's own OR case volume vanishes with the comparison; the user reads a missing count as punishment.
- Any future division view that names surgeons in a subspecialty under five undercuts the anonymity promised on their own page.
- O/E is rendered as a point estimate in a bar chart; a surgeon with one death reads 1.7 as a verdict.
- The wRVU as-of date is not prominent; the current month reads as a collapse.
- M&M "on pace" is computed without approved leave applied; a false off-pace flag appears.
- The surgeon-attributable cancellation reason set is decided by engineers rather than periop leadership; a cancellation counts against a surgeon on a rule nobody signed.
- A surgeon disputes a delay reason expecting the FCOT number to move; it does not, because the institutional count is by wheels-in time. The dispute form must say this before the surgeon files (J2.2).

**Open questions for the brief's owner:**
- FCOT grace window: the brief assumes none; this document shows the institutional definition verbatim so the number matches periop's. Confirm.
- Peer-under-five: does "a number is not shown" hide the surgeon's own value or only the comparison? Default here: own value stays, comparison is withheld with the reason.
- Peer-group counting rule: roster members or surgeons who clear min-n; viewer excluded; per-site rows for multi-site surgeons. Default here is the design doc's rule.
- Opt-out: is pilot peer-spread participation voluntary, and what happens to a group of five when one surgeon opts out?
- Non-attendance at step zero: does it remove a surgeon from colleagues' comparison groups, or only withhold the spread from that surgeon? Default here: withhold the spread only; the surgeon stays in peer denominators.
- Sustained delay-reason dispute and FCOT: does it change the number? Default here: no; the number stays the institutional count so it matches periop's, and the corrected reason shows on the row. The alternative, excluding non-surgeon delays from an "as adjudicated" FCOT, gives a number that does not reconcile to periop's and would need its own label (see J2.7, J4.5).
- M&M pace rule: confirm the rule proposed in J1.14.
- Adoption signal in the email months: reply and interview (default), or a tracked link that needs a host?
- wRVU: mirror only, with no charge-level record list and no dispute action; corrections go to the billing office. Confirm, or ask for a charge-level list with CPT and wRVU per date of service.
- Trend form for rolling-12-month and quarterly metrics (J1.16 to J1.19): quarterly points with intervals for rates, monthly line for access and clinic metrics. Confirm.
- Are in-progress months ever shown? Default: closed months only.
- Should the block utilization tile, once the feed exists, read "not applicable" for a surgeon with no block, or be absent?
- wRVU: confirm "no target" is intended; if so, confirm the tile wording "your comp target is in your comp letter; this page does not show it".
- Does M&M attendance show a trend? The ground rule says every metric does; its "Shown as" lists none.
- Mortality O/E at min-n 30: keep on the individual view with an interval, or move to division and site views?
- Form of the spread for share and count metrics (dots, bars, box).
- Trend window (rolling 12 months, fiscal year to date, all history).
- Export or print for annual review, and what the chief will hold (the OPPE packet is outside this system).
- Super-long boarder threshold (assumed more than 30 days) and whether LOS history is restated when confirmed.

## J2 — A record is wrong: dispute it, the chief rules on the record not the score, and the outcome shows up in the list

**Primary actor:** Attending neurosurgeon.

**Secondary actors:** Division chief (adjudicator); department chair (when the chief is involved); department analyst (recompute and reload); periop analytics contact and other feed owners (source corrections); the re-credited surgeon (Dr. Y).

**Trigger:** The month-three email shows First-case on-time start (FCOT) at about 60% against a trend that has sat near 85% for a year, and the surgeon's marker sits at the low end of the site spread. The surgeon suspects a data problem before a behavior problem and opens the case list.

**Frequency:** Disputes are expected several times a month across the pilot in months one to three as surgeons check their lists for the first time, then occasional. Each dispute is a trust event, not an exception path. The design doc's trust criterion: at least two disputes filed by surgeons who are not the brief's owner, each resolved within 14 days with the outcome visible in the record list, and the disputed-record rate per surgeon falling from month one to month three.

**Preconditions (data that must exist):**
- A published period with record lists (J1.4) and a dispute action on every row of every list.
- A dispute ledger (a shared sheet is enough for four metrics) with the fields in J2.2.
- Roster mapping each surgeon to their division chief, and naming the chair.
- A named periop analytics contact who has agreed to receive the monthly sustained-override list.
- Definitions as versioned code so each dispute records the version the number was computed under.

Every decision has a decider, a date and a note. The surgeon's row shows the outcome, including a denial. The tile shows "as logged" and "as adjudicated" side by side when an attribution decision moves the count; a delay-reason decision changes the row and leaves the count matching periop's, and the surgeon was told so before filing. The trend point is restated with a marker, and periop receives one monthly list of sustained overrides. In the email months every outcome reaches the surgeon as a one-record decision email inside 14 days. A re-credited surgeon sees the record with its history and can dispute in turn. Zero "the number is just wrong" outcomes: every attribution error a surgeon finds is reproducible from the case list.

**Failure modes (what goes wrong and what the user sees):**
- The chief's queue goes unwatched; disputes sit open for weeks; the row says "with chief" forever and the surgeon concludes disputing is theater (Premise 6).
- A sustained dispute is "fixed" by hand-editing the extract; the next refresh overwrites it and the error returns. Corrections live in the ledger (annotated) or at source (corrected), never in the extract.
- The chief has no annotated outcome available and periop will not change the field; the dispute ends in an email and nothing changes.
- Re-attribution moves a case onto Dr. Y's list silently; Dr. Y finds a case they did not lead and files a counter-dispute that is not linked to the first.
- The involvement tests are applied by hand and the chief adjudicates a case that would be re-credited to themselves.
- Recompute flips the metric across min-n (from shown to "not shown: 3 first cases; needs at least 4") and the surgeon reads the disappearance as punishment; the tile must name the dispute as the cause.
- Disputes are wired only to FCOT rows because only they carry a delay reason; surgeons cannot dispute a Duration estimate accuracy or OR case volume row although the brief says any record can be disputed.
- A definition disagreement is filed as a record dispute and has nowhere to go.
- A decision changes a month already in the trend, but history is not recomputed; the trend and the list disagree.
- The disputed number is already suppressed under min-n and the record list is not openable, so the surgeon cannot find the record to dispute. Record lists must open even when the number is suppressed (proposed default).
- A QI-sourced correction is entered into the scorecard by hand because the QI coordinator is slow; the next QI load overwrites it and the complication rate changes twice.
- A sustained delay-reason dispute is built as an exclusion from FCOT; the adjudicated number stops reconciling to periop's and two numbers reach the same faculty meeting.
- In the email months a reply sits in the dispute mailbox unentered; the surgeon sees nothing until the next monthly email and concludes nothing happened.
- A denied dispute leaves no trace on the row; the surgeon cannot tell a denial from a dispute nobody read.

## J4 — Close and publish a period: reconcile to periop, version the definitions, suppress with reasons, publish

**Primary actor:** Department analyst (data steward).

**Secondary actors:** Periop analytics contact (feed owner); definitions owner; division chief (receives the small-group suppression summary before step zero); later feed owners; attending neurosurgeon (hand-off to J1).

**Trigger:** Part A: a month closes and the periop case-level extract arrives through the ticketed request. Later, a quarter closes, or the fiscal year, with more feeds. Part B: one of the brief's five "to be confirmed" items is confirmed, a pending data source is registered, a configuration value changes, or a roster fact changes (site, subspecialty, faculty status, leave, block, direct leader, chief).

**Frequency:** Part A monthly, from a one-command documented procedure on a standing calendar entry; a missed run shows as a stale "last refreshed" date on every artifact. Part B irregular: several versions in year one; roster changes whenever a surgeon joins, leaves, moves or takes leave.

**Preconditions (data that must exist):**
- Periop case-level extract (surgeon by panel and role, room, scheduled and actual times, booked duration, delay reason events, cancellation reason code) with at least 12 months of history, plus periop's own surgeon-level FCOT and cancellation report for the same months. Ticket number recorded.
- Dated department roster as a slowly changing dimension.
- Definitions as code: one function per metric with min-n, peer rule, definition text and version, matched to the institutional definition.
- A named analyst on a chair-approved time fraction (design doc survival criterion).

**Success outcome:** Every record for the period is credited to exactly one clinician by the rule for its type, with exceptions listed and the shared flag visible. The four wedge numbers match periop's report or carry a written definition delta before any surgeon sees them. Every number carries its definition version, its record ids, its exclusion counts and its as-of date. Every suppressed cell carries a reason with counts. No peer identity reaches surgeon-facing data. A named analyst who is not the builder has run the batch unassisted for two consecutive months from the one-command procedure.

**Failure modes (what goes wrong and what the user sees):**
- A feed fails and a metric publishes as zero instead of a reason.
- Numbers are stored without a definition version id; after a version change the drill-down shows the wrong definition for old periods.
- The old version is edited in place; published numbers link to text that no longer says what they were computed under.
- A version change restates old periods silently; a surgeon's FCOT history moves with no boundary on the trend.
- Block allocation and release minutes are assumed to ride in the OR-log extract; the metric is computed from missing data.
- An OR-log case with a blank or multi-surgeon primary field is silently dropped or double-credited.
- A Vizient admission with no index operation is credited to the discharging attending, contrary to the rule, or dropped without a trace.
- A QI event recorded after quarter close restates a rate; the trend shows a restated point with no marker.
- A roster error puts a surgeon in the wrong peer group; the spread and the peer-under-five decision are both wrong.
- A pending-source metric is backfilled from a source that lacks the attribution join key.
- The M&M scaled target is applied before the leave-scaling assumption is confirmed and a surgeon is flagged "cannot reach 8" on an unconfirmed rule.
- The reconciliation gate is skipped under time pressure; two FCOT numbers reach the same faculty meeting.
- The batch is run only by the builder; the system dies at the first reorg.

**Metrics touched:** All 25 items in the brief at load and compute time (J4.4 for the wedge four; J4.10 for the rest), plus the three division and site measures as "not computed in this cycle" (J4.9).

**Ground rules exercised:** 1 (J4.2, J4.10, J4.13), 2 (J4.9: dispute address and override list), 3 (J4.3, J4.6, J4.12, J4.16), 4 (J4.3, J4.7), 5 (J4.4, J4.11, J4.14, J4.15), 6 (J4.4, J4.14).

**Open questions for the brief's owner:**
- Who confirms an institutional definition and who approves a new version: the owner alone, the chiefs, the chair?
- Are earlier periods restated under a new version or frozen at their version, and is the boundary shown the way J4.14 proposes?
- Are surgeons notified when a definition changes?
- Where does the roster come from and who maintains it?
- Should a period publish when a feed loaded partially, and with what wording?
- Which system supplies new-patient visits, note timestamps, third-next-available samples, referral-received dates, ICU transfers, faculty status and approved leave?
- Index-operation rule for admissions with zero or multiple department operations; encounter-to-case join across entities.
- Restrict QI-database comparisons to the site whose database it is until a shared registry exists; consider NHSN for Surgical site infection?
- LOS: whole-encounter days or post-operative days?
- Multi-site surgeons: per-site rows only, or also a pooled row?
- Does the reconciliation gate ever accept an adjudicated FCOT that excludes non-surgeon delays? Default here: no; a delay-reason override changes the row, not the count.

## Journeys considered and not chosen

- **Weekly evening dashboard check (surgeon-first J1):** assumed unprompted weekly browsing, an in-progress-month display the brief does not define, and using the scorecard to find unsigned notes the EHR already flags. Its wRVU, M&M and block-utilization tile details were folded into J1 Part B and J1.9.
- **Comp-plan 1:1 walking the full scorecard (leadership-first J1):** framed the tool as comp accountability including wRVU, which the brief gives no target and the design doc forbids as a system of record; depended on a chief-facing full scorecard the brief never describes and the design doc sequences after the surgeon-first 30-day gate. Its session-level M&M list and readmission drill-down survive in J1.14 and J1.15.
- **Division and site views (leadership-first J3):** the brief names three metrics and nothing else; every screen would be invented; out of scope for the first milestone. Its step 4 (what a small subspecialty will and will not see) survives as the chief summary in J4.6.
- **Direct leader reads the inbox first and coaches (leadership-first J4):** the leader reading before the surgeon breaks the surgeon-first rule; the read-and-coach steps and leader-of-record failure modes were folded into J3.8 to J3.10.
- **Fiscal-year-end twelve-metric tour (surgeon-first J4):** a surgeon preparing for review reads three numbers, not twelve; its distinct suppression reasons, the 25-day admission, the partner's reoperation and the final-vs-pending read became J1 Part B with the interval treatment and the hard mortality case added. - **Surgeon verifies every number without asking anyone (data-trust-first J3):** an eleven-step bucket tour with no trigger; its suppression wording, "not applicable" state, division-and-site page text and dispute-on-every-row rule are in J1 and J4.
- **Close a period across all twelve feeds in one run (data-trust-first J1 as written):** the platform-first pattern; narrowed to the wedge in J4 Part A with the other feeds as J4.10.
- **Monthly OR scorecard for one subspecialty at one site with sign-in, block utilization in and cancellations out (narrowest-wedge J1 as written):** conflicted with the revised wedge on four points; its record-list columns, row-count invariant, tolerance-test display and success signal are in J1.

- **A separate definition-versioning journey (derivability judge's recommended J4):** merged into J4 Part B as the same actor's second trigger, per the same judge's fallback advice.
