## Problem Statement

A neurosurgery department inside MGB has written a v0.2 spec for ~25 per-surgeon metrics across six buckets (volume, efficiency, access, quality, patient experience, citizenship). The metrics draw on at least twelve institutional sources once the pending ones are counted (OR log, block allocation and release schedule, Vizient CDB encounter-level extract, department QI database, survey vendor extract, professional billing, clinic scheduling, referral work queue, ADT unit transfers, HR leave, a dated department roster, and a new QR-code M&M attendance system). Today a surgeon sees a few of these numbers about themselves, each from a different owner with its own definition: patient-experience scores twice a year at faculty meeting, wRVUs in the comp letter, periop efficiency numbers when block is reallocated, and OPPE numbers when the medical staff office sends a packet. None of them comes with the case list behind it, and none has a dispute path that changes anything.

**What is actually being built (restated after the framing check):** not a scorecard, and not a competing number. There is no composite score, no rank, and no target except M&M attendance. It is a read-only, per-surgeon metrics viewer that reconciles to the institutional numbers that already exist and adds what their owners cannot give a surgeon: (a) a record-level attribution ledger behind every number, (b) a dispute path with a written outcome that shows up in the record list, (c) minimum-n and minimum-peer-group suppression that explains itself, (d) anonymous peer spread, and (e) trend on every metric. The charts are the surface. The ledger and the dispute path are the product. It is never a comp or OPPE system of record; it mirrors those numbers read-only with their source and date on the tile.

**Framing check (three axes):**
- Language precision. "Scorecard" implies a score; the brief has none. "Peer group" is undefined in counting rule (roster members or surgeons who clear min-n; with or without the viewer) and in site for multi-site surgeons. "Anonymous" is not defined against re-identification: with five peers and visible volumes, everyone knows who is who.
- Hidden assumptions. (1) Each feed is obtainable at surgeon-level attribution by this department; (2) one primary surgeon per case is the norm in academic neurosurgery (it is not for multi-panel spine and skull-base cases); (3) the QR attendance system exists and will be live for the fiscal year; (4) a surgeon will open something they were not required to open; (5) a dispute that ends with the chief changes something.
- Real vs hypothetical. The brief is a spec written in the second person ("you can move it by"). No surgeon is quoted. The only evidenced pain is contractual (comp plan counts M&M attendance) and cadence ("available any time instead of twice a year").

**Failure patterns named:** platform-first (twelve feeds and 25 metrics before one surgeon sees one real number); hypothetical user; assuming interest equals demand for the quality bucket; two numbers for the same metric (a department FCOT that differs from periop's loses, because periop's controls block); a "developmental" tool read by faculty as a comp input in waiting; and the documented report-card failure mode (risk aversion, case-selection gaming, mistrust of attribution) that the brief's ground rules are designed against but have not been tested against a real surgeon.

## Constraints

- No composite score, no ranking, no target except M&M attendance (brief).
- Never a comp or OPPE system of record. Comp- and OPPE-relevant numbers are a read-only mirror with source and as-of date on the tile. This sentence is shown to faculty on the "what this is not" page.
- Surgeon first: a surgeon sees any number at least 30 days before any leader view of it exists. Leader, division and site views are out of scope for A.
- Attribution: exactly one clinician per record (brief). Primary surgeon from the OR log with a deterministic tie-break in code for multi-panel cases and a visible "shared" flag on the record; index surgeon for admissions under a written index-operation rule; rendering provider for visits under a named field; named provider for surveys (brief; field choices are Open Questions).
- Suppression, written as code-level rules: peer group = dated-roster members with the same site (and subspecialty where the brief says so) as of the period, excluding the viewer; a spread renders only if at least five peers each clear that metric's min-n; per-site metrics for multi-site surgeons; every suppressed cell shows the rule that suppressed it, never a blank (brief). The counting rule is the doc's reading of the brief and is an Open Question.
- Anonymity: peer spread only, never identities (brief). Feedback inbox visible to the surgeon and direct leader only; comments never counted or rolled up (brief).
- Disputes: a dispute is a ledger record (case id, field, surgeon claim, adjudicator, decision, date, definition version). Two outcomes: "source corrected" (routed to the feed owner, re-ingested) or "annotated" (record stays as logged; the chief's note and an override flag are shown; the metric shows "as logged" and "as adjudicated" counts side by side). Overrides are visible, versioned, never silent. Sustained overrides go to periop monthly as a correction request; the department does not wait for them.
- Complications come from the QI database only, never hand-entered (brief); QI-sourced rates are labeled "self-reported, unadjusted" (the brief's own label rule for metrics without a risk model).
- Uncertainty (B): any O/E or rate metric renders with an interval; the spread is an interval or funnel chart, never a bar chart of point estimates; a surgeon whose interval includes the peer median is labeled "no evidence of difference."
- wRVU: self-comparison only, no peer, no target (brief); months shown "as of <date>, may increase" with the prior snapshot ghosted and the restatement delta logged. Vizient, survey and billing metrics carry their own per-source as-of date and model version.
- Patient experience: labeled on screen with the survey scope it comes from (outpatient provider items; confirm whether any inpatient item carries provider attribution).
- Unresolved definitions (brief-flagged, plus review): FCOT grace window and room eligibility (first scheduled case in room where the surgeon is primary); surgeon-attributable delay reason set; surgeon-attributable cancellation reason set; booked vs actual duration fields (scheduled procedure length vs in-room interval); index-operation rule and encounters with no department operation; readmission observed/expected universe; visit attribution field, new-patient rule, note timestamp; wRVU table year, modifier treatment, date-of-service vs posting basis, comp office source query; super-long boarder threshold; referral-received source and proxy; ICU-return proxy; M&M leave-scaling; peer-group counting rule; multi-site and multi-subspecialty roster handling.
- PHI-derived, surgeon-identified data inside a hospital: information security review, data governance sign-off, a QI-vs-research determination, and (for quality and complication data) a medical staff office decision on peer-review privilege are prerequisites. The wedge stays on efficiency metrics, which are operational, to keep the first governance ask small.
- Hard gate for this run: design only, no code.

## Prior Art and Conventional Wisdom

Search ran with generalized category terms only.

- Layer 1 (tried and true): OPPE/FPPE is a Joint Commission requirement, and attribution accuracy is what gives peer review credibility (Credentialing Resource Center). Vizient CDB already produces physician-level mortality, LOS, readmission, and complication O/E with drill-down (Vizient CDB). Surgeon-specific report cards reliably cause risk aversion and case-selection gaming when results are published or ranked (Health Affairs 2015; NY cardiac surgery literature). At low per-surgeon volumes, registry data rarely detects individual outliers (statewide collaborative study). Applied here: a surgeon with 30 admissions and 2% expected mortality has 0.6 expected deaths; one death is an O/E of 1.7. Hence the uncertainty constraint above.
- Layer 2 (current discourse): balanced scorecards tied to comp; "if RVUs update daily while quality lags by months, volume will always win"; email attachments reduce engagement versus on-demand portals (accepted, and overridden for months one to three because no host exists yet); anonymous FPPE processes are eroding OR trust (57% of surveyed surgeons avoided high-risk cases in relation to anonymous reporting).
- Layer 3 (first principles, from this brief): the brief already contains the corrections the literature asks for (O/E where a risk model exists, "unadjusted" labels, min-n, no rank, dispute path, drill to records). Where conventional wisdom is wrong here: the anonymous peer bar chart. In a five-surgeon group where everyone knows each other's volume, anonymity is fake; it removes the useful conversation without preventing identification. Recorded as Premise 5.

## Premises

Provisionally accepted for this run. Each is falsifiable. Dispute any of them and the design changes.

1. Periop analytics will deliver, through its standard request process (ticket number recorded), its surgeon-level efficiency report and the case-level extract behind it (surgeon by panel and role, room, scheduled and actual times, booked duration, delay reason events, cancellation reason code) with at least twelve months of history. The ticket's lead time is part of the calendar, not the build.
2. The share of OR-log cases with more than one attending in a primary role is unmeasured and is measured in the Assignment before any rule is chosen. Until then the brief's one-clinician rule holds, with a deterministic tie-break in code (first-listed primary panel) and a visible "shared" flag; crediting both attendings is an Open Question for the brief's owner. If the rate exceeds 5% in any subspecialty, "handle by dispute" is withdrawn.
3. Hospital quality analytics (the institutional Vizient owner) will provide an encounter-level Vizient extract with expected values, model version and a hospital encounter ID; the OR-log ledger supplies the encounter-to-index-surgeon crosswalk via a written index-operation rule. Vizient lags two to three months and restates expected values on model refresh, so quality metrics carry their own as-of date.
4. All neurosurgeons at the pilot site form a peer group large enough (roster to confirm) for the three site-compared wedge metrics to render. Whether any subspecialty at that site reaches five is measured, not assumed; the two subspecialty-compared metrics may suppress.
5. Anonymous peer spread in groups of five to eight does not prevent re-identification, so it does not deliver the protection it promises. (Eureka candidate; dispute this if the department's experience says otherwise.)
6. Surgeons accept a number they can trace to a record list and dispute, and only if a sustained dispute visibly changes the record list. A dispute that ends in an email to the chief and nothing else teaches surgeons that disputing is theater.
7. Emailing a surgeon their own numbers and their own case list to their MGB mailbox needs no new host or governance path (to confirm with the privacy office in week one). Hosting a surgeon-identified page needs information security, data governance, a QI determination and, for quality data, a medical staff office peer-review decision; their lead times are unobserved and are an Open Question, not an estimate.
8. The system survives its champion only if a named department analyst role runs the monthly batch on a standing, chair-approved time fraction, and the department's own recurring outputs (semiannual patient-experience slide, annual faculty review packet) are generated from its ledger. It is never a comp or OPPE system of record; if the chair will not fund the analyst fraction, build nothing beyond the email.
9. Twice-a-year delivery of patient-experience scores is a felt pain; more frequent access will be used more than twice a year. No evidence either way.
10. Doing nothing has a real cost: faculty-meeting time spent arguing about whose number is right, and chief hours assembling numbers for comp and OPPE conversations without record-level backing. Both unmeasured.
11. Participation in the pilot peer spread is voluntary per surgeon. An opted-out surgeon still receives the self-only view and is excluded from every peer group; the spread renders only if five participating peers clear min-n. (Opt-out is not addressed by the brief; Open Question.)
12. For quality O/E metrics, point estimates against a peer spread at the brief's min-n produce noise that reads as signal. Any O/E renders with an interval and a "no evidence of difference" default; whether mortality belongs on the individual view at all is an Open Question for the brief's owner.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: One site, one owning team, email-first, four metrics (minimal viable)
- Summary: monthly batch reads the periop case-level extract, applies metric definitions written as code (one function per metric with its min-n, peer rule, definition text and version, matched to the institutional definition), writes an attribution ledger (every case with surgeon, panel role, shared flag, source, version, dispute state) and emits one email per surgeon to their MGB mailbox: four numbers with trend, their own case list (date, room, procedure, scheduled start, wheels-in, booked vs actual, delay reason, cancellation reason), "why not shown" reasons, a reply-to-dispute address, and a "last refreshed" date. Disputes land in a minimal dispute table (a shared sheet is enough for four metrics) with the two outcomes defined in Constraints. Month one self-only; month two adds the anonymous spread for surgeons who attended the ground-rules presentation. A hosted per-surgeon view is a separate deliverable gated on governance, built either as a thin app layer that maps SSO identity to the surgeon record or on the enterprise BI tool with row-level security (see C).
- Effort: build S (human 3-4 weeks; AI-assisted about 1 week once the extract is in hand). Calendar: periop data request plus privacy-office confirmation for email, unknown; hosted view adds security review, data governance and QI determination, unknown.
- Risk: Medium technically (record-level drill-down is PHI; the hosted view needs per-user authorization, and "static files behind SSO" would have given authentication without authorization); Medium on feed access; calendar risk unknown.
- Pros: real surgeons see real numbers inside a month of the extract landing; no new host and no new approval for the first three months; the number matches periop's, so there is nothing to argue about at faculty meeting except the case list; exercises every ground rule on low-stakes metrics; definitions-as-code, the ledger schema and the dispute table carry forward to B.
- Cons: no roles beyond surgeon and chief; no feedback inbox; no cross-feed metrics; email reduces engagement versus a portal (accepted for now); anonymity of a five-peer spread is weak (Premise 5); trend needs history the extract may not carry; the dispute table is a sheet, not a workflow.
- Reuses: the institutional definitions verbatim as docstrings; periop's existing report as the reconciliation target; the existing dispute route (chief, then chair); MGB email.
- Completeness: 5/10 (one bucket, suppression, drill-down and dispute outcomes done properly).

### Approach B: Attribution ledger, metric engine, role-based web app (ideal architecture)
- Summary: nightly ETL from all twelve sources into the record-level ledger; a metric engine that computes every metric in the brief from the ledger with versioned definitions and per-source as-of dates; a web app with surgeon, direct-leader, chief, chair, division, and site views; dispute workflow with audit trail and the two outcomes; feedback inbox with private notes and a de-identification step; M&M pace tracker; wRVU self-tracker with restatement display; interval charts for every O/E and rate metric.
- Effort: XL. Human 6-12 months with an analyst and an engineer; AI-assisted 2-3 months of build, but the calendar is set by twelve feed owners and governance, not code.
- Risk: High. Twelve feed owners, PHI governance and peer-review privilege, the encounter-level Vizient extract and the encounter-to-case join, the QR system not yet existing, and cross-site QI databases with different capture rules.
- Pros: the thing the brief describes; disputes and drill-down are native rather than bolted on; the department's own recurring outputs can be generated from it (Premise 8).
- Cons: no surgeon sees anything for months; greenlight has to be won on a promise; every unresolved definition blocks a bucket; highest chance of dying in a reorg before launch.
- Reuses: Vizient CDB encounter-level expected values; the QI database as sole complication source; the patient survey feed in its existing faculty-meeting shape; everything built in A.
- Completeness: 9/10.

### Approach C: Build inside the enterprise BI tool the department already has (lateral)
- Summary: implement the metric views as dashboards in the BI or Epic-adjacent reporting tool MGB already licenses and governs. Distribution, SSO, PHI governance, row-level security and drill-to-record are already solved there.
- Effort: M. Human 6-10 weeks; AI-assisted 2-4 weeks, gated on tool access.
- Risk: Medium.
- Pros: fastest path to sanctioned hosting; per-user authorization and drill-to-row-level detail are standard in Tableau and Power BI and are exactly what a static A cannot do; analysts already know the tool; periop and Vizient feeds may already be connected. The first draft dismissed C on drill-down, which was wrong.
- Cons: dispute workflow, private notes and the feedback inbox are genuinely weak in BI tools; "every number links to its definition" needs discipline the tool does not enforce; tool lock-in; definitions can drift into the BI team's hands unless the metric code stays the single source.
- Reuses: enterprise SSO, hosting, RLS, and possibly existing Vizient and billing connections.
- Completeness: 6/10 (charts and authorization strong, dispute mechanics weak). For the wedge, A and C are equal on disputes (both use a table outside the tool), so C is the leading candidate for A's hosted view.

Ruled out: a composite score or rank (the brief forbids it and the literature says it causes case-selection gaming). Starting with the quality bucket (highest stakes, weakest attribution, smallest n, peer-review privilege; wrong place to meet surgeons for the first time). Static per-surgeon pages behind SSO (authentication without authorization for PHI). A department-computed FCOT that differs from periop's (two numbers for the same metric).

## Recommended Approach

Approach A as revised: one site, all neurosurgeons, periop's case-level extract, four metrics, delivered by email for months one to three, with the hosted view built on the enterprise BI tool (C, for hosting only) once governance clears, and the definitions-as-code and ledger schema shared by both as the first module of B.

- Why A: the intrapreneurship goal is a greenlight from someone outside the department. A sponsor greenlights what they can see working with real surgeons' data, and the fastest real data is the extract behind a report periop already produces. Matching periop's number and adding the case list, the delay reason, self-explaining suppression and a dispute with a written outcome proves the brief's ground rules before asking for feeds two through twelve.
- Why email first: no new host, no new approval, and it tests whether surgeons value the record list on its own before any peer spread exists. The engagement cost of email is accepted for three months.
- Why not B first: months of no visible output is how internal tools die before launch.
- Why not C alone: dispute outcomes and private notes are the product and BI tools do them badly; but C is the right home for the hosted, authorized view, so the hybrid keeps the trust mechanics in the ledger and the charts in the tool.
- Build order inside A: ledger schema, metric functions and dispute table first (they are reused); reconciliation to periop's report second (the numbers must match before any surgeon sees them); email third; hosted view when governance lands.
- Sequencing for trust: faculty-meeting presentation of definitions and ground rules before any peer data renders; month one self-only; month two peer spread for attendees only; opt-out honored per Premise 11.
- Anonymity rule for A: follow the brief (hide under five peers) but log how often each spread renders; that number decides Premise 5.
- No wireframe was produced in this run (headless, no-code rule). A 1-3 screen rough sketch of the email and the case list should precede /plan-design-review.

## Open Questions

For the brief's owner (each would change an explicit decision in the brief, so the doc records the question rather than the change):
- Disputes vs "displays what the system of record logs" (Bucket 6): does that rule apply to every bucket, and if so, is an adjudicated override shown beside the logged value acceptable? This doc's position: show both.
- Co-surgeon cases: keep "exactly one clinician per record" with a tie-break and a shared flag, or credit both attendings in volume and exclude shared cases from FCOT and duration accuracy? Measured rate in the Assignment decides urgency.
- FCOT definition: the brief assumes no grace window pending the institutional definition. This doc adopts the institutional definition verbatim so the number matches periop's. Confirm.
- Readmission: observed "to any MGB hospital" against a Vizient expected value that is hospital-scoped inflates every O/E by construction. Use Vizient's system-level readmission module for both, or compute observed from Epic across MGB and label it "unadjusted" per the brief's own rule?
- Mortality O/E at min-n 30 (0.6 expected deaths at 2%) cannot support individual inference. Keep it on the individual view with an interval, or move it to division and site views alongside turnover time?
- QI-database complications are compared "across the system" but each department's QI database has its own capture rules, and SSI has an institutional NHSN source. Restrict to the site whose database it is until a shared registry exists, and consider NHSN for SSI?
- LOS: the brief says whole-encounter days; a patient admitted to medicine and operated on day five credits five pre-op days to the surgeon. Whole-encounter or post-operative?
- wRVU is comp-linked and shown with no target while M&M is comp-linked and shown with one. Confirm intended; if so the wRVU tile says "your comp target is in your comp letter; this page does not show it."
- Peer-group counting rule: roster members or surgeons who clear min-n; viewer included or excluded. This doc reads it as peers excluding the viewer, at least five each clearing min-n.
- Opt-out: is pilot peer-spread participation voluntary, and what happens to the group when one surgeon opts out (Premise 11)?
- Multi-site surgeons: per-site rows only, or also a pooled "all sites" row?
