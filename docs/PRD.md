## 1. Summary

The Clinician Scorecard is a read-only, per-surgeon metrics viewer for a neurosurgery department inside MGB, covering the 25 items in the brief across six buckets: volume and mix, efficiency, access, quality and outcomes, patient experience, and citizenship. Behind every number it keeps a record-level attribution ledger, a dispute path with a written outcome that shows up in the record list, suppression that says why a number is missing, an anonymous peer spread, and a trend. It ships first as a monthly email carrying four OR-log metrics and the surgeon's own case list, and grows one feed at a time as each feed's owner and governance gate clears.

The one-line product statement, from the design doc: **not a scorecard, and not a competing number.** There is no composite score, no rank, and no target except M&M attendance. It reconciles to the institutional numbers that already exist and adds what their owners cannot give a surgeon: the case list, the dispute, the reason, the spread and the trend. The ledger and the dispute path are the product; the charts are the surface.

The brief (v0.2, 2026-09-09) is owned by Omar Arnaout. The brief is preserved verbatim; edits go in a new version, not in place.

## 2. Background and problem

**Status quo.** A surgeon today sees a few numbers about themselves, each from a different owner with its own definition: patient-experience scores twice a year at faculty meeting, wRVUs in the comp letter, periop efficiency numbers when block is reallocated, and OPPE numbers when the medical staff office sends a packet. None comes with the case list behind it, and none has a dispute path that changes anything. The design doc's per-bucket inventory (Status Quo table) lists the expected owner and definition for each; owners and definitions are expected, not confirmed, and confirming them is part of the Assignment (section 13, M0). The real competitor is the periop report plus the OPPE packet plus the faculty-meeting slide, held side by side at a meeting where two numbers for the same surgeon disagree.

**Demand evidence and its gaps.** The design doc tags demand as UNKNOWN, partially answered from the brief for comp-linked metrics. The brief quotes a comp-plan link ("Target: 8 of 12 per fiscal year, per the comp plan") and a cadence complaint ("available any time instead of twice a year"). No surgeon is quoted. Nobody is on record disputing a periop or OPPE number, keeping a spreadsheet, or asking when this ships. The demand this system can own, per the design doc, is "see my own list before anyone else does" (the surgeon) and "stop spending faculty-meeting time arguing about whose number is right" (the chair). Evidence that would change the position: a surgeon who disputed a number in the last year and had nothing to point at; a chief who spent more than two hours on one surgeon's numbers; a surgeon keeping their own spreadsheet. The Assignment (M0) is designed to find that evidence or its absence before code.

**Cost of doing nothing.** Faculty-meeting minutes spent arguing about whose number is right, and chief hours assembling numbers for comp and OPPE conversations without record-level backing. Both are unmeasured (P10).

**Why now.** Three things line up: a written v0.2 brief with an owner and six ground rules that already contain the corrections the report-card literature asks for (O/E where a risk model exists, "unadjusted" labels, min-n, no rank, dispute path, drill to records); a new QR-code M&M attendance system tied to the comp plan; and a periop analytics team that already produces a surgeon-level report whose case-level extract is the fastest real data available. The design doc's warning stands: the report-card failure mode (risk aversion, case-selection gaming, mistrust of attribution) is what the ground rules are designed against, and they have not yet been tested on a real surgeon.

**Evidence M0 collects before code (added by the CEO review's outside voice; section 13, M0).** Five 20-minute conversations with surgeons across subspecialties and two with division chiefs, one question each: "tell me about the last number about you that you thought was wrong; what did you do?" One meeting with periop analytics and the enterprise analytics team asking three questions: does periop's surgeon-level report have a case-level drill; can a surgeon-scoped view of it be published, with row-level security, in the tool they already run; which of the brief's 25 items already exist in an institutional dashboard. One question to periop analytics: what they want back for shipping the extract, signing the reason sets and receiving the override list. The answers are recorded in this section as a new version of this document, with the count of surgeons who described a specific disputed number. Nothing here asserts what the institution runs; the questions exist because no document confirms it (OQ-47, OQ-54).

## 6. User journeys

Full text: `docs/01-user-journeys.md`. Four journeys, 60 steps (J1: 20; J2: 14 including J2.3a and J2.8a; J3: 10; J4: 16). Step IDs are stable and cited throughout.

**J1. Month posted (20 steps).** The surgeon attends step zero, hears the definitions and the "what this is not" page, and decides whether to opt out. The month-one email arrives on a phone with four tiles, a trend, the definition version and their own case list; the surgeon finds the late first case from the 14th and sees why it counted under the institutional definition. Month two adds the anonymous spread for attendees, with the peer count shown; a surgeon who recognizes a colleague is not surprised, because step zero said so. Part B (J1.13 to J1.20) walks the later buckets at quarter close and annual review: the wRVU mirror with its as-of date, M&M pace, Length of stay (O/E) with an interval and an excluded count, the hard mortality case (35 admissions, one death, 0.6 expected, "no evidence of difference"), the QI-sourced rates labeled "self-reported, unadjusted", and which numbers are final and which are waiting.

**J2. A record is wrong (14 steps).** The month-three FCOT sits at 60% against a year near 85%. The surgeon opens the list, disputes three records individually (a wrong delay reason, a blank reason, a case Dr. Y led), and the form says before filing what a sustained dispute on that field will do. The chief sees the record and its provenance, not a score, and rules; the analyst recomputes the same day. A sustained delay-reason dispute corrects the row and leaves the count at 6 of 10 so it still reconciles to periop; the sustained re-attribution moves the count to 6 of 9 "as adjudicated" beside 6 of 10 "as logged". A denial leaves its note on the row. Periop receives one monthly list of sustained overrides. In the email months (J2.3a) the analyst enters replies within two business days and sends a one-record decision email inside 14 days.

**J3. What patients said (10 steps).** The surgeon opens Bucket 5 in the faculty-meeting layout any time instead of twice a year, sees a hidden month with its reason ("Hidden: 7 responses this month; needs at least 10"), opens the per-response record list, reads the Patient feedback inbox newest first with same-survey scores and nothing counted, leaves a private note, and disputes a response that was an APP's visit on the provider-named field only. The direct leader reads the same inbox no earlier than 30 days later, sees no notes and nothing aggregated, and coaches from the comment.

**J4. Close and publish a period (16 steps).** The analyst runs one command: stages the extract with its ticket, checks field presence, reviews the attribution exception list and the co-surgeon rate, computes peer groups from the dated roster, computes the four metrics with version stamps and the row-count invariant, runs the reconciliation gate against periop's report, reviews the suppression report (100% reasons), writes only anonymous spreads, checks feed health (never a zero), publishes one email per surgeon and one override list to periop. Part B (J4.11 to J4.16) lands a confirmed definition as a new version, registers a pending source, recomputes only the effective periods with a boundary on the trend, and records a roster change with dated peer-group recompute.

**Coverage claim.** Every one of the 25 items in the brief has at least one cell in the journeys' coverage matrix, and the three division-and-site-only measures appear as page text and as "not computed", which is all the brief specifies for them. All six ground rules are exercised in every journey (journeys "Ground rules by journey"). Every journey step maps to at least one feature (catalogue "Journey step traceability"); the two steps with no system behavior of their own (J1.11, J3.9) are covered by acceptance criteria on the features listed there.

### 10.1 Architecture summary

The system is one Python package with one CLI and one PostgreSQL database, built around the attribution ledger. Institutional extracts arrive as files by ticket, are loaded verbatim into a `raw` schema, typed into `src`, and credited to exactly one clinician per record by a versioned rule (GR1). Metric definitions are Python files, one per version, registered by content hash, so an edit to a registered file is refused and a confirmed item is a new file beside the old (D3). A monthly `scorecard close` computes every number, suppression reason, peer spread and trend marker into the ledger and refuses to publish unless the wedge numbers reconcile to periop's report (D6), every cell has a value or a worded reason (GR3), and every record list has as many rows as its denominator (GR5). Surfaces only render what the ledger holds: a plain-text email with the surgeon's own case list in months one to three, one server-rendered web app behind MGB SSO from M2, and an optional pre-suppressed export for the enterprise BI tool. A dispute is a ledger row pinned to one record, routed by executable tests, decided with one of four outcomes, and, when sustained, applied as an override that recomputes the surgeon's numbers under an "as adjudicated" basis beside the "as logged" one (D7) and goes to the feed owner as a correction request. Every write and every view lands in an append-only audit log. The analyst runs it from one CLI on a standing calendar entry (P8). Full text: technical design "Overview" and "Data flow, including the dispute and recompute loop".

### 10.4 Data sources and ingestion

Every feed goes through the same six loader steps: fingerprint, receive, allowlist and raw, field check, type and record, attribute (technical design "Ingestion: one loader per feed"). Three rules hold for all thirteen:

- Idempotency: the file's sha256 is unique per feed; loading the same file again is a no-op and writes nothing. A corrected file is a new load that supersedes the old by `source_key`; superseded rows stay, and a published number still points at the load it was computed from, so a reload never silently changes a number (R-100). Nothing in `raw` or `src` is ever edited; the loader role has no UPDATE (R-05, D7).
- Field presence, not proxies: each loader declares the required columns per metric; a missing column marks the dependent metrics "not computable: <field> not in extract" and the load continues (R-83). Columns not on the feed's allowlist are dropped before `raw` and named in the staging summary (PS-1).
- As-of dates come from the owner's document, never the load date (R-101). A missing feed publishes a reason, never a zero (R-85); "last refreshed" is the publish date, so a missed month is visible by construction (R-86).

### B. Glossary

Terms are the technical design's ("Glossary") where the two documents share one; the meaning here is the product-level one.

| Term | Meaning here |
|---|---|
| Annotated | A sustained dispute outcome where the record stays as logged and the adjudicator's note and an override flag are shown; the metric shows "as logged" and "as adjudicated" side by side |
| Artifact | One rendered output for one surgeon and period: the monthly email body and CSV, a decision email, a page render, an export row set. Every surface renders from the same object |
| As logged / as adjudicated | The value from the source as loaded and attributed, and the value after sustained overrides are applied; see Basis |
| As-of date | The feed's own date, taken from the document the owner sent, never the load date; distinct from "last refreshed" |
| Attribution | The ledger row that credits a record to exactly one clinician under a named rule version (GR1); exactly one current row per record. An adjudicated crediting is an override read beside it, not a second attribution |
| Attribution exception | A record the rule could not credit (blank, ambiguous or out-of-department primary; no index operation; unmapped provider); held until the analyst assigns or excludes it with a reason; publish is blocked while any is unresolved |
| Attribution ledger | The record-level store every number is computed from: records, attribution, overrides, definition versions, metric values with their record links, suppression decisions, peer snapshots, disputes, runs, deliveries and the audit log |
| Basis | Which crediting and field values a number was computed under: logged (the source as loaded and attributed) or adjudicated (the logged frame with every active override applied). Adjudicated rows exist only where an override touches the cell; a tile shows both only when they differ (D-11) |

| Cell | One (clinician, metric, period, site) a surface could render; every cell has a value or a suppression decision on both the value and the comparison scope, or the run fails |
| Convergence | The loader step that compares each open correction request with the re-ingested field on the next load of the owning feed and retires the override, with history, when the source now carries the adjudicated value |
| Correction request | The row a sustained decision writes when the feed owner has a named contact; grouped per feed owner into the monthly override list; open until a load confirms it |
| Definition delta | A written, authored explanation of a difference between the brief's assumption and the institutional definition, or between the department's number and periop's report; the only thing besides a sustained re-credit that can explain a reconciliation difference |
| Definition parameter | A named value on a definition version (grace window minutes, super-long boarder days, a signed reason set) with a status of assumed, confirmed or pending sign-off, the brief's value kept beside the value in force; changing one is a new version |
| Definition version | One immutable file per metric version, registered by the hash of its bytes, with definition text, parameters, cadence, min-n, comparator, effective period and confirmer; every number carries the id it was computed under and old periods keep theirs |
| Direct leader | The one person besides the surgeon who may read the Patient feedback inbox; whether this is the division chief is OQ-41 |
| Dispute | A ledger row pinned to one record and one field, filed by the credited surgeon, routed once by executable tests, decided once by the named adjudicator; never a dispute of a number, tile, spread or period |
| Effect notice | The sentence, stored per definition version and disputable field, that tells the surgeon before filing what a sustained dispute changes (F-56) |
| Feed | One institutional source with a key, a loader, a file contract, a required-field list, an as-of capture rule and an attribution rule; thirteen are named (section 10.4) |
| Feed load | One staged file with its hash, ticket number, as-of date, model version, row count, field checklist and status; the same file loads once; a corrected file is a new load that supersedes the old |
| Field checklist | Per load, which required columns were present; a missing column makes the dependent metrics "not computable" rather than computed from a proxy |
| Gate | A check that stops a run or a send (reconciliation, feed health, row count, no blank cell, exceptions, channel gate, payload scan, own records, grants; the technical design's G1 to G18). A governance gate is a recorded sign-off row with a signer, a reference and a second identity's confirmation that opens a channel or a bucket |
| GR1 to GR6 | The brief's six ground rules as numbered in the journeys |
| Index operation | The department operation that credits an admission to its surgeon, under a written rule (OQ-31) |
| Last refreshed | The publish date of the run being rendered, the only source of the date on every artifact, so a missed month shows the previous month's date |
| Minimum n (min-n) | The smallest count at which a metric's number is shown; below it the screen says why |
| O/E | Observed divided by expected, where Vizient supplies the expected value; 1.0 is exactly as expected |
| Override (record override) | The row a sustained decision writes: the disputed field, its logged value, its adjudicated value, the decider, the note and the definition version. The only mutation layer; source rows are never edited; retires, never deletes, when the source converges |
| Override list | The monthly list per feed owner of unconfirmed correction requests (source ids and fields only), sent under its own privacy gate (F-64) |
| Peer group | Dated-roster members with the same site (and subspecialty where the brief says so) as of the period, excluding the viewer (proposed default, OQ-07) |
| Peer snapshot | Per viewer, metric, period and site: the peer count, how many clear min-n, the viewer's eligibility, whether the spread rendered, the sorted peer values and the viewer's own value and position; member identities are held apart from it; every snapshot is a render-log entry (F-36) |
| Peer-under-five | The rule that a comparison is withheld when fewer than five peers each clear min-n |
| Period | A closed month, quarter, rolling 12 months or fiscal year, per the metric's cadence, named by a period label from which dates are derived (D-49) |
| Provenance panel | What an adjudicator sees with a dispute: the record's columns as the surgeon saw them, source and load date, attribution rule, definition version, the stored value of the disputed field, prior disputes; never a score (F-52) |
| Reconciliation gate | The publish check that compares each wedge number with periop's own surgeon-level report loaded as a feed; any difference must be a written definition delta or a sustained re-credit, or nothing publishes (D-15) |
| Record ref | The opaque, type-prefixed token that is the only record id a surgeon ever sees; it resolves to a source record only inside the ledger under a database role (D-19) |
| Restatement | A new metric value row that replaces a current one, linked to the row it restates and described by one of eight reason categories (late record, override, version change, model refresh, wRVU snapshot and the rest); the trend marks the point as restated; nothing is overwritten |
| Roster (dated roster) | A slowly changing dimension read "as of" a date: site, subspecialty, chief, direct leader, faculty dates, block, step-zero attendance, opt-out. Peer groups read it as of the period end; access as of today; routing as of the filing date |
| Route | Where a dispute goes at filing: chief, chair (an involvement test fired), direct leader (survey records only, and only if the owner turns the setting on under OQ-43), or held when no route is defined |
| Run | One row per command that writes: kind, period, code version, active definition versions, gate results, status, publish date; the period record of section 7.13 |
| Shared flag | A visible mark on a multi-panel case credited to the first-listed primary surgeon |
| Source corrected | A sustained dispute outcome routed to the feed owner; the annotation applies until re-ingest confirms the change |
| Source registry | Per metric, whether its source is pending confirmation, not in this release, or registered with a system, extract, join key, attribution rule and effective period (F-103) |
| Spread | The anonymous peer comparison: sorted peer values and the viewer's own marker, never identities; rendered only with five peers each clearing min-n and an eligible viewer |
| Step zero | The faculty meeting at which definitions, ground rules and the "what this is not" page are presented before any peer data renders |
| Suppression decision | The row written per cell and scope every run, including "none", with the kind, the catalogue template, the counts and the rendered reason text; the mechanism behind "the screen says why instead of showing a blank" |
| Viewer | The clinician a snapshot, decision or artifact is rendered for; peer groups exclude the viewer; the hosted view scopes every query by the viewer resolved from SSO and the roster |
| Wedge | The first release: one site, all neurosurgeons, periop's case-level extract, four metrics, by email for months one to three |
| Wedge four | OR case volume; First-case on-time start (FCOT); Duration estimate accuracy; Same-day cancellations you could have prevented |
| "What this is not" | The versioned statement: not a comp input, not a rank, not an OPPE record; visible to you; your chief sees a record only when you dispute it; no leader view in the pilot; disputes change the record list |
